# MYSTERY OS — ANTI-CLICHE / ANTI-TEMPLATE POLICY v0.1

**Status:** INCUBATION DRAFT  
**Date:** 2026-09-17

## 1. Purpose

This policy prevents MYSTERY OS from producing cheap, overused, mechanically familiar crime/mystery fiction even when the manuscript is technically coherent.

The rule is not “never use a trope”. Tropes are genre language. The rule is: **do not substitute a trope for an original dramatic decision.**

A familiar device is acceptable only when at least one of the following is true:

- execution materially reverses or deepens the expected function;
- the device emerges naturally from distinctive characters and setting;
- the trope is deliberately used to misdirect while a different causal story is operating underneath;
- the familiar surface is transformed by an original consequence, motive, structure or emotional meaning.

Changing names, occupations, cities or supernatural creatures does not count as reinvention.

## 2. Enforcement levels

### BLOCKED BY DEFAULT
May not enter an approved StoryDefinition/Architecture unless a human accepts a written reinvention rationale showing why this exact use is necessary and non-generic.

### HIGH-RISK TROPE
Allowed, but the system must test for predictability, market saturation and similarity to the author's/series' prior work.

### STYLE PATHOLOGY
A prose/dialogue pattern that becomes synthetic, cheap or repetitive through wording or frequency.

### SERIES COLLISION
A device may be acceptable once but prohibited again inside the same series if it would create formula fatigue.

## 3. BLOCKED-BY-DEFAULT mystery twists

- “the detective/protagonist was the killer all along” without a rigorously fair narrative design;
- secret identical twin / unknown sibling introduced late to rescue an impossible plot;
- “the victim was not really dead” with no strong prior evidentiary basis;
- “everyone did it” used as a borrowed prestige twist rather than an organically built solution;
- culprit is the least-developed character solely because readers would not suspect them;
- culprit is the obvious creepy loner because the plot has no deeper causal design;
- “it was all a dream / hallucination / coma” as the main explanation;
- sudden psychiatric diagnosis used as a universal explanation for violence or supernatural events;
- amnesia deployed mainly to hide information the viewpoint character would otherwise know;
- confession conveniently replaces proof;
- dying victim names/indicates the killer through an implausibly cryptic final message only because the genre expects a puzzle;
- hidden passage/secret room appears only when the solution needs it;
- surveillance/camera/phone/battery/network fails at exactly the necessary moment without causal setup;
- previously unmentioned forensic fact appears in the final chapter and decides the case;
- coincidence identifies the culprit after investigation fails;
- murderer keeps trophies/photos/news clippings in an absurdly discoverable shrine unless character logic truly requires it;
- culprit explains the entire plan to the hero while delaying an easy kill/arrest escape;
- “the supernatural entity made the human do it” as a replacement for motive and agency;
- a prophecy/vision states the answer in disguised form and the ending merely decodes it.

## 4. BLOCKED-BY-DEFAULT supernatural cliches

Especially important for `Линия 112` and other contemporary mystical mysteries:

- inherited old mansion/house as automatic gateway to evil;
- dusty diary/letters/newspaper archive as the primary mechanism for explaining the mystery;
- secret room behind a bookcase used without strong fresh function;
- haunted mirror/doll/music box/portrait/antique object used in default form;
- child draws the monster/ghost and adults ignore it until too late;
- lights flicker, clock stops, temperature drops and electronics fail as generic proof of presence;
- ghost communicates only through conveniently cryptic fragments because full information would end the book;
- Latin/ancient-language inscription that conveniently explains the curse;
- generic occult symbol/pentagram placed at the scene solely to signal “dark ritual”;
- mysterious old woman/keeper who knows everything but refuses to explain for no credible reason;
- local legend repeated as exposition and then reproduced literally;
- “this land was cursed long ago” without specific causal/social meaning;
- entity attacks exactly according to horror timing rather than declared rules;
- exorcism/ritual suddenly solves a phenomenon whose mechanics were never established;
- protagonist discovers at the climax that they are uniquely gifted/chosen by bloodline;
- convenient psychic who can retrieve whatever clue the plot currently lacks;
- repeated dead-person phone call/message trope as a fixed series engine unless the entire concept has a substantially original rule system.

These elements are not forbidden from world literature. They are blocked as **default production choices** because MYSTERY OS must not reach for them merely because they are recognizable shortcuts.

## 5. HIGH-RISK investigator cliches

Require differentiation and a reason they improve this specific story:

- brilliant detective who is rude to everyone but always right;
- alcoholic/divorced/burned-out detective whose damage exists only as genre decoration;
- investigator with tragic dead spouse/child used as instant depth;
- hyper-observant “human lie detector” who infers truth from tiny gestures with unjustified certainty;
- amateur sleuth who repeatedly discovers bodies by coincidence;
- journalist/podcaster who gains police-level access with no credible mechanism;
- coroner/hacker/IT friend who supplies any required information on demand;
- police chief who obstructs the hero solely to create conflict;
- all official investigators are stupid while protagonist alone sees the obvious;
- protagonist ignores basic safety/procedure so the climax can happen;
- quirky habit/food/pet substituted for an actual personality.

## 6. HIGH-RISK culprit/victim cliches

- abusive spouse as automatic first/true suspect;
- jealous lover with no dimension beyond jealousy;
- business partner kills for money in the simplest imaginable arrangement;
- serial killer whose motive is a generic childhood trauma;
- “religious fanatic” or “occultist” as shorthand for evil;
- mentally ill character used as danger shorthand;
- sex worker/addict/homeless person exists only as disposable victim/information source;
- rich family where every member is generically corrupt;
- victim turns out to have “a dark secret” that is only a bag of unrelated shocking behaviors;
- killer constructs elaborate themed murders for no reason beyond author spectacle;
- genius mastermind predicts every human action perfectly.

## 7. HIGH-RISK plot engines

- locked room solved by a mechanism readers have seen countless times;
- serial murders copying zodiac/seven deadly sins/tarot cards without a distinctive causal reason;
- reunion/wedding/holiday gathering where a conveniently snowed-in group starts dying;
- reality show/influencer retreat where “everyone has secrets” but characters remain interchangeable;
- true-crime podcast reopens an old case solely because this is fashionable;
- cold case and present murder mirror each other exactly;
- “the next victim is the protagonist” used as automatic third-act escalation;
- kidnapped family member forces investigator off-book solely to isolate them;
- corrupt-police conspiracy expands whenever the plot needs another obstacle;
- elite secret society responsible for everything;
- memory card/USB/phone containing the decisive answer becomes a chase MacGuffin;
- villain targets investigators with riddles instead of pursuing a credible goal.

## 8. Relationship cliches

Mystery does not get a free pass for cheap romance.

Blocked/high-risk depending on severity:

- instant hostility that exists only to convert into romance;
- “he is dangerous but only she sees his soft side” without credible behavior change;
- jealousy manufactured through easily solved misunderstanding;
- two adults refuse one normal sentence for 150 pages to preserve romantic tension;
- investigator/suspect romance where ethical/legal consequences are ignored;
- dead spouse exists solely to prevent current intimacy until a formulaic healing scene;
- love interest repeatedly rescues competent protagonist, reducing their agency;
- sexual tension replaces relationship development;
- final kiss/reconciliation treated as mandatory reward after solving the case.

## 9. Cheap atmosphere and horror shorthand

Flag repeated/default use of:

- “ледяной ветер пробежал по спине” equivalents;
- unexplained goosebumps as supernatural detector;
- “тишина была слишком громкой” style pseudo-poetic horror shorthand;
- moonlight, fog, ravens, black cats, candles, thunder used as automatic darkness coding;
- every scary place smells of dampness, dust and decay;
- mirrors used for jump scares;
- footsteps stop when hero stops;
- shadow moves at edge of vision repeatedly without progression;
- door closes by itself as generic supernatural beat;
- character says “здесь что-то не так” instead of the scene demonstrating what is wrong.

One such detail can be normal description. Repeated reliance becomes a STYLE PATHOLOGY.

## 10. Dialogue cliches

Flag or reject:

- villain: “мы с тобой не так уж различаемся”;
- “если я тебе расскажу, ты не поверишь” used to postpone exposition;
- “ты должен мне поверить” without supporting action;
- “это невозможно” repeated after the story has already established the supernatural;
- “у нас мало времени” as generic urgency;
- “я могу всё объяснить” followed by interruption solely to delay information;
- investigator recites facts both speakers already know for reader exposition;
- every suspect speaks in ominous half-sentences;
- witness withholds basic information for “dramatic” reasons no person would accept;
- characters name each other's emotions instead of behaving.

## 11. Prose / AI-template pathologies

MysteryBench must inspect frequency and location of:

- repeated `не X, а Y` constructions;
- pseudo-aphoristic one-line paragraphs used for fake weight;
- artificial triples of adjectives/actions/revelations;
- generic sensory packages reused across scenes;
- every chapter ending with a rhetorical teaser;
- excessive fragments to simulate tension;
- “она ещё не знала, что...” omniscient bait in otherwise limited POV;
- repeated “всё изменилось” / “это было только начало” type cliffhanger language;
- characters constantly “замирают”, “сглатывают”, “сжимают кулаки”, “задерживают дыхание” as emotion shorthand;
- generic cinematic camera prose detached from viewpoint;
- decorative metaphors that do not belong to character voice;
- explanatory paragraphs after dialogue that restate what the scene already showed;
- author moralizing about trauma/evil instead of dramatizing consequences;
- perfectly symmetrical scene/chapter lengths and beats that reveal machine templating.

Findings are contextual, not blind word bans. Frequency, distinctiveness and function matter.

## 12. Series-level anti-template rules

Within one series, the following are tracked as consumable or collision-prone assets:

- type of opening crime/event;
- victim profile;
- culprit relationship to victim;
- core motive family;
- murder/concealment mechanism;
- supernatural mechanism/entity/rule;
- central false hypothesis;
- signature red-herring type;
- midpoint reversal type;
- protagonist-in-danger mechanism;
- final reveal staging;
- setting type;
- emotional conflict between recurring leads;
- romantic progression beat;
- ending image/beat.

A later book may echo a prior asset only when the Series Editor records why recurrence is intentional and materially different.

“Same plot, different victim/profession/location” is a BLOCKING Series Collision.

## 13. Anti-cliche review questions

Before StoryDefinition approval:

1. Which three parts of this idea are immediately recognizable genre tropes?
2. If all proper names are removed, does the concept still have a specific identity?
3. Which element would a seasoned mystery reader predict first?
4. Are we selecting any device because it is easy for the model to generate?
5. Does the case depend on a stock motive, stock trauma or stock supernatural object?
6. Can the same premise be summarized by naming two popular tropes and nothing more?
7. What concrete causal choice makes the book ours?
8. Does the proposed novelty change the story, or only the decoration?

Before Literary Master:

1. Which scenes read like scenes we have encountered in dozens of genre books?
2. Which twist would be spoiled by a reader simply knowing common trope conventions?
3. Which characters are functions rather than people?
4. Where does dialogue exist only to delay information?
5. Which supernatural beats could be replaced with any generic ghost scene without changing plot?
6. Which prose patterns repeat often enough to feel generated?
7. Does any book in this series already use the same structural trick?

## 14. Exception protocol

A BLOCKED-BY-DEFAULT device can be proposed only with an `AntiClicheException` containing:

- exact trope/cliche;
- why it is necessary;
- expected reader familiarity;
- how this use transforms the convention;
- what would be lost by replacing it;
- comparison with prior books in the same series;
- human decision: `ACCEPT | REWORK | REJECT`.

AI cannot approve its own exception.

## 15. Principle

MYSTERY OS should never congratulate itself merely for being “unexpected”. Randomness is easy. The quality target is **specific, credible, emotionally alive and causally earned fiction that does not feel assembled from genre leftovers**.
