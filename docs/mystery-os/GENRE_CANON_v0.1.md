# MYSTERY OS — GENRE CANON v0.1

**Status:** INCUBATION DRAFT  
**Date:** 2026-09-17

## 1. Purpose

This canon defines reusable quality rules for mystery/crime fiction. It is intentionally stricter than a trope list. It governs causal integrity, reader fairness, mystery construction, character logic, pacing, supernatural use and originality.

Subgenre profiles may adapt presentation, violence level, romance proportion, procedural depth and reveal style. They may not silently waive causal integrity, earned-solution requirements, anti-cheat rules or the anti-cliche policy.

## 2. Foundational rule: case truth exists before narrative concealment

The production system must know what objectively happened before deciding how to conceal, distort or reveal that truth.

Required separation:

- objective event history;
- culprit intent;
- actual outcome;
- witness perception;
- what each suspect knows;
- what each suspect says;
- what investigator(s) infer;
- what the reader has actually been shown.

Narrative ambiguity is allowed. Production ambiguity about decisive causality is not.

## 3. Fair-play principle

The reader need not notice or correctly interpret every decisive clue, but the manuscript must not require indispensable information withheld until the solution speech.

Before final acceptance, the system identifies:

- which earlier clues support the solution;
- where they appear;
- which alternative interpretations were plausible;
- which clues distinguish the correct solution from major alternatives;
- which facts merely confirm the solution after it is already inferable.

### BLOCKING fair-play failures

Unless the declared genre profile explicitly rejects fair play and the reader promise reflects that:

- culprit is introduced only at the end;
- decisive twin/relative/identity substitution appears without prior basis;
- final explanation depends on a room, passage, device, witness or rule never available earlier;
- investigator reveals off-page decisive evidence unavailable to the reader;
- supernatural ability appears for the first time in the climax and solves the case;
- the case is solved solely because the culprit confesses;
- critical witness information is artificially omitted from narrated scenes only to preserve surprise;
- a previously impossible timeline is repaired with an unseeded exception.

## 4. Mystery is not confusion

The reader should normally understand:

- what question is being investigated;
- why it matters;
- the meaningful current hypotheses at appropriate points;
- what changed when a clue or revelation landed.

Complexity may increase. Orientation should not collapse. Confusion caused by undefined facts, interchangeable characters or withheld basic context is not suspense.

## 5. Causal solvability

For every accepted solution, the system checks:

### Motive
The motive need not be morally reasonable but must be psychologically and situationally credible for this character.

### Means
The culprit must possess or plausibly obtain required capability, tool, knowledge or access.

### Opportunity
Time, location, access, witnesses and constraints must permit the act.

### Concealment
If the culprit avoids immediate detection, the concealment method must be credible and itself produce risks/traces.

### Error / unavoidable trace
A solved case normally contains something the culprit overlooked, misunderstood, could not control or was forced to risk. “Perfect because the author says so” is not accepted.

## 6. Suspect design

A material suspect should contribute multiple real functions such as:

- plausible motive;
- access/opportunity;
- contradictory behavior;
- a real secret unrelated to the core crime;
- a meaningful relationship to victim/case;
- evidence supporting suspicion;
- evidence later weakening/excluding suspicion;
- pressure on protagonist or other suspects.

No fixed suspect count is universal. Three strong suspects may outperform eight interchangeable names.

Suspects must be differentiated by motives, social position, risk, voice, relationships and narrative function—not merely names and occupations.

## 7. Secrets and lies

Every significant lie requires a reason. The system records:

- truth concealed;
- speaker goal;
- perceived disclosure risk;
- lie told;
- trace/inconsistency left by the lie;
- who can detect it;
- whether it relates to the core crime or a separate secret.

Repeated lying merely to prolong the manuscript is a MAJOR defect.

## 8. Clue taxonomy

MYSTERY OS distinguishes:

- **Direct clue:** materially supports a case proposition.
- **Corroborating clue:** strengthens another clue/hypothesis.
- **Exclusion clue:** rules out or weakens suspect/time/mechanism/hypothesis.
- **Recontextualized clue:** appears with one plausible meaning and later gains another without changing the underlying fact.
- **Behavioral clue:** meaningful action, omission, knowledge, language or reaction; must not become authorial mind-reading.
- **Negative clue:** something expected is absent; the expectation itself must be established.
- **Supernatural clue:** produced by the supernatural layer; reliability/limits must be declared.
- **Atmospheric detail:** supports tone/setting and may not be retroactively promoted into a decisive clue unless causal relevance genuinely existed.

## 9. Clue placement

No universal percentage formula is authoritative. The system checks distribution instead:

- meaningful evidence exists well before the final act;
- clues are not all clustered immediately before the solution;
- investigation materially evolves rather than loops;
- early details receive later payoff or are intentionally non-clue texture;
- decisive evidence is not buried in unreadable exposition solely to claim technical fairness;
- repeated clue reminders do not make the solution trivial.

Clues should preferably do more than one job: advance drama/character/setting while remaining causally real.

## 10. Red herrings

A red herring is not random misinformation inserted to delay the reader.

Every material red herring must come from a real cause:

- a character hides something real but non-culpable;
- genuine evidence admits a plausible wrong interpretation;
- the culprit deliberately manipulates evidence and can actually do so;
- an observer makes an understandable error;
- two causal chains overlap.

When a red herring collapses, its underlying reality must still make sense.

## 11. Reveal and twist rules

A twist must change the reader's model of events while preserving prior facts.

The system rejects twists that work only by:

- contradicting previously established objective facts;
- hiding the viewpoint character's conscious knowledge without justified narrative form;
- introducing an unseeded identity/person/place/rule;
- turning a character irrational solely to force the twist;
- relying on coincidence at the exact moment a solution is needed;
- using shock value without causal consequence.

A predictable but earned reveal is preferable to an impossible surprise. The target is surprising **and** retrospectively inevitable.

## 12. Supernatural discipline

Supernatural mystery remains causally disciplined.

If supernatural phenomena materially affect the case, the system must know:

- what can happen;
- what cannot happen;
- under what conditions;
- who can perceive it;
- whether messages/visions can lie, distort or be incomplete;
- what physical consequences can occur;
- what cost/risk exists;
- which rules the reader must encounter before those rules become decisive.

“Magic did it” is not a solution. A supernatural phenomenon may create the impossible condition, but the resulting mystery still requires earned causality.

## 13. Character causality

Characters may surprise the reader; they may not behave stupidly only to preserve the plot.

For material decisions, the system should be able to answer:

- what the character knows;
- what they want;
- what they fear;
- what constraints apply;
- what alternatives they perceive;
- why the chosen action is locally rational or emotionally credible.

Repeated “failure to communicate” without a character-specific reason is a quality defect.

## 14. Investigator competence

The protagonist need not be a professional detective, but the narrative must establish why this person can plausibly investigate and continue investigating.

Unacceptable by default:

- protagonist repeatedly enters obvious danger only because the plot needs a scene;
- authorities ignore clear evidence without a credible institutional/person-specific reason;
- amateur investigator receives unlimited confidential access without cause;
- protagonist solves specialized forensic/procedural problems through unexplained genius;
- all professionals are made incompetent solely to make the hero look intelligent.

## 15. Scene architecture

Each scene must change something material. Valid work includes:

- change case state;
- change a hypothesis;
- reveal/recontextualize evidence;
- create danger/constraint;
- alter a relationship in a way that affects choices;
- force a decision;
- seed/pay off a promise;
- materially deepen setting/atmosphere while advancing another function.

Pure transit, recap, duplicated interrogations and circular internal monologue are candidates for compression/deletion.

## 16. Pacing

No mandatory beat percentages are universal.

The system looks for:

- an opening that establishes crime/mystery pressure early enough for the declared subgenre;
- escalating cost/uncertainty;
- periodic changes in the working model of the case;
- variation between discovery, confrontation, consequence and reflection;
- no long section where the investigation generates no meaningful new state;
- adequate buildup before denouement rather than a rushed solution lecture.

Slow-burn atmosphere is allowed. Boredom is not a genre virtue.

## 17. Denouement

The ending must answer the main book-level mystery questions promised to the reader.

The denouement should:

- demonstrate the causal chain;
- connect decisive clues;
- explain major false trails;
- show why leading alternatives fail;
- resolve immediate stakes;
- preserve only intentionally declared residual ambiguity.

It must not become a chapter-length dump of facts never dramatized or made inferable.

## 18. Commercial originality

Genre familiarity is useful; formulaic sameness is not.

Before writing, the system asks:

- what familiar reader promise is being delivered;
- what is genuinely specific/distinctive here;
- what would remain if names/occupations/location were swapped;
- whether the premise can be described without relying only on trope labels;
- whether the protagonist, case mechanism, emotional engine and setting have independent identity;
- whether the same outline could be reused for another series with cosmetic substitutions.

If the answer is “yes, mostly cosmetic”, the concept fails the originality gate.

## 19. Anti-cliche policy is part of canon

`ANTI_CLICHE_AND_TEMPLATE_POLICY_v0.1.md` is binding on concept, architecture, drafting and final QA.

Cheap cliches and tired formulas are not treated as harmless style issues. They can be structural defects because they make plot, characters and prose predictable.

The policy distinguishes:

- **BLOCKED BY DEFAULT:** too stale/cheap to use without an explicit human-approved reinvention rationale;
- **HIGH-RISK TROPE:** allowed only when execution materially transforms expectations;
- **STYLE PATHOLOGY:** repeated wording/scene patterns that make prose synthetic or derivative;
- **SERIES COLLISION:** reuse of a device already consumed by the same series.

A cliche may not be “fixed” by merely changing nouns, profession, city or supernatural creature.

## 20. Professional external principles incorporated

MYSTERY OS aligns with durable crime-writing practice reflected in professional guidance: crime/mystery identity should be legible from the opening; characters and context should be memorable; tension/conflict must propel the story; chronology should be checked; clues and red herrings should perform real work; and the ending should answer the central who/what/when/where/why/how rather than dump every answer on the final pages.

These are principles, not rigid templates. The system protects the outcome, not one universal outline.
