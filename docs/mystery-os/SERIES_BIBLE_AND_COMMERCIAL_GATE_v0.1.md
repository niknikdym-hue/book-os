# MYSTERY OS — SERIES BIBLE AND COMMERCIAL GATE v0.1

**Status:** INCUBATION DRAFT  
**Date:** 2026-09-17

## 1. Principle

A mystery series is not “the same book with a different corpse”. The recurring engine must create familiarity while each volume delivers a materially new case, emotional movement and reading experience.

MYSTERY OS separates:

- **series constants** — what readers return for;
- **series variables** — what must change book to book;
- **consumable assets** — twists/mechanisms that lose value when repeated;
- **reserved assets** — ideas deliberately held for later books;
- **forbidden collisions** — repetitions that would make the series formula visible.

## 2. Commercial Gate before story architecture

The system does not promise sales. It performs bounded commercial due diligence before committing to a full manuscript.

### Required questions

- Who is the target reader?
- Which current/evergreen shelf/category does the book belong to?
- What reader promise is familiar enough to understand quickly?
- What concrete feature makes this book specific rather than generic?
- Is the premise explainable in 1–2 sentences without lore dump?
- Does the opening situation naturally generate suspense?
- Can the book title/cover/annotation communicate genre and hook quickly?
- Does the premise support text and audio?
- Is this a one-book idea or a repeatable series engine?
- What comparable works/series prove audience existence?
- What is deliberately **not** copied from those comparables?
- Is the market crowded with the exact same gimmick?
- Does the book still have identity if trendy trope labels are removed?

### Gate result

`GO | REWORK | HOLD | NO_GO`

AI proposes evidence and diagnosis. Human/Owner accepts the commercial direction.

## 3. Series Bible

A `MysterySeriesBible` should contain at minimum:

### Identity
- series title;
- genre/subgenre;
- reader promise;
- tone;
- age/content rating target;
- setting model;
- supernatural contract if any;
- visual/cover language;
- audio strategy.

### Recurring cast
For each recurring character:
- stable identity;
- role in series engine;
- competencies/limits;
- voice;
- long arc;
- relationship states;
- facts that cannot change without explicit revision;
- secrets reserved for later books.

### Series engine
Describe why new cases can arise without absurd coincidence.

The engine must answer:
- why these people encounter/investigate cases;
- what access they plausibly have;
- what makes different cases possible;
- what would cause the engine to feel contrived;
- what kinds of cases are outside scope.

### World / reality rules
- real-world baseline;
- supernatural reality mode;
- recurring supernatural laws/entities if any;
- institution/procedural boundaries;
- technology baseline;
- geography and recurring locations.

### Long arc
- series-level mystery questions;
- character relationship progression;
- revelations reserved for future volumes;
- maximum planned dependency between books;
- stand-alone resolution requirement per volume.

## 4. Book Passport

Each volume has a `MysteryBookPassport` summarizing its uniqueness relative to the series.

Fields:
- book number/working title;
- primary case type;
- victim/target profile;
- culprit relationship to victim;
- motive family;
- means/mechanism;
- concealment mechanism;
- primary clue pattern;
- primary red-herring pattern;
- supernatural phenomenon/rule used;
- core setting type;
- emotional arc;
- recurring relationship movement;
- midpoint reversal type;
- climax/reveal staging;
- ending state;
- assets consumed;
- assets newly reserved.

## 5. Difference Map

Before approving a new volume, compare it against every prior volume at least across:

- premise;
- crime/case type;
- culprit role;
- motive;
- mechanism;
- concealment;
- suspect architecture;
- clue architecture;
- supernatural device;
- midpoint turn;
- protagonist jeopardy;
- emotional conflict;
- final reveal;
- location/atmosphere;
- prose/scene pattern risks.

Similarity may be lexical or semantic. Renaming professions/locations does not clear a collision.

## 6. Series collision rules

### BLOCKING collisions

- same culprit relationship and motive with cosmetic changes;
- same murder/impossible mechanism;
- same supernatural mechanism used as the central mystery again;
- same “trusted ally is culprit” reveal pattern repeated closely;
- same false-suspect ladder in same order;
- same protagonist-kidnapped third act used again;
- same secret-past revelation driving another book;
- same final confrontation staging with names/setting swapped;
- same emotional fight/reconciliation beat between recurring leads;
- same case solution architecture despite different surface premise.

### Allowed recurrence

Recurring series signatures are welcome when they are **promises**, not copied plots:

- recurring leads;
- shared city/world;
- recognizable tone;
- investigative institution/team;
- recurring supporting cast;
- recurring supernatural ontology with new consequences;
- recurring thematic concern;
- signature humor/voice;
- consistent level of darkness.

## 7. Stand-alone vs serial dependency

Default commercial rule for mystery series:

- each volume resolves its primary case;
- recurring relationships/series mystery may continue;
- reader should be able to enter through a later volume with bounded orientation;
- prior-book spoilers should be minimized when possible but not at the expense of absurd vagueness.

A strongly serialized exception is allowed only if StoryDefinition and market positioning explicitly choose it.

## 8. Series fatigue monitor

After each released/accepted volume, assess:

- repeated opening pattern;
- repeated body-discovery pattern;
- repeated interview cadence;
- recurring cast becoming static;
- escalation inflation (“bigger serial killer every time”);
- supernatural power creep;
- romance stalled artificially;
- repeated cliffhanger language;
- repeated cover/title syntax;
- reader feedback pointing to predictability.

Series success is not permission to freeze the formula.

## 9. Market evidence discipline

Commercial research may use:

- platform category/bestseller/series pages;
- publication dates;
- visible ratings/review counts as engagement signals, not sales truth;
- publisher catalogues;
- reader reviews and community discussions;
- audio presence;
- price/length/series count;
- search interest when available;
- comparable-cover/title patterns.

Do not claim sales from ratings unless actual sales data is available.

Record source date because market conditions change.

## 10. Title / cover / annotation gate

Before release, assess:

- title distinctiveness;
- collision with existing major titles/series;
- immediate genre signal;
- cover legibility at thumbnail size;
- series branding consistency;
- whether title/cover promise the book actually delivered;
- annotation hook without spoilers;
- absence of empty hype and generic cliches.

## 11. Audio-commercial gate

When audio is selected:

- hook must survive without visual formatting;
- recurring characters need audibly distinguishable identities/names;
- case complexity should remain followable on first listen;
- screen/text-message clues require natural spoken handling;
- pronunciation ledger is prepared;
- chapter openings/closings should not depend on visual tricks.

## 12. Human decision

Commercial analysis informs the Owner. It does not autonomously choose what the Owner must publish, price or market.
