# MYSTERY OS — QUALITY ROUTING / SELECTIVE PREMIUM INTELLIGENCE v0.1

**Status:** INCUBATION DRAFT  
**Date:** 2026-09-17

## 1. Governing rule

MYSTERY OS inherits the accepted BOOK OS principle:

> **Use the best proven executor for the specific editorial operation, not one expensive model for the whole book.**

The target is professional literary quality with disciplined spend.

Premium reasoning budget should be concentrated where mistakes contaminate hundreds of downstream pages or where whole-book judgment materially exceeds routine drafting requirements.

## 2. Three execution classes

### Class A — Deterministic / local first

Use no model when software can establish the property reliably.

Examples:
- schema validation;
- staleness propagation;
- timeline arithmetic and overlap detection;
- character-knowledge dependency checks from structured state;
- clue lifecycle completeness;
- exact repetition;
- scene/chapter length/counts;
- authority/version binding;
- series asset collision by exact IDs/categories;
- cost/provenance checks;
- export integrity.

Do not spend premium tokens to rediscover deterministic defects.

### Class B — Standard editorial intelligence

Use the least expensive proven Writer/Editor capable of the operation without material quality loss.

Typical operations:
- bounded scene drafting from a strong SceneContract;
- routine scene revision;
- continuity extraction;
- summarization into structured memory;
- mechanical dialogue tightening after higher-level decisions are fixed;
- low-risk transition repair;
- formatting/adaptation work;
- first-pass classification of findings;
- routine audio adaptation under an accepted script contract.

This class may produce most manuscript words.

### Class C — Premium / global intelligence

Use a stronger frontier reasoning/writing executor, Astra-class lane, or bounded Agents API editorial-prep run when expected quality gain is material.

Typical operations:
- commercial series positioning and premise competition;
- Series Bible / recurring engine design;
- selection among materially different concept architectures;
- CaseSolution design and repair;
- motive/mechanism redesign;
- suspect-field architecture;
- clue/reveal architecture;
- complex MysticRuleSet design;
- ending architecture;
- character/relationship long-arc design;
- anti-cliche adversarial critique;
- midpoint structural diagnosis;
- whole-book developmental diagnosis;
- adversarial case reconstruction;
- series-level semantic collision analysis;
- difficult voice/literary diagnosis;
- final high-level publication-readiness review.

Premium lane is selective by design; it should not write every ordinary scene merely because it is stronger.

## 3. Why premium effort is concentrated upstream

A weak sentence can be repaired locally.

A weak CaseSolution can corrupt:
- every clue;
- every suspect scene;
- the timeline;
- the midpoint;
- the ending;
- character motivations;
- hundreds of thousands of characters of prose.

Therefore upstream architecture has a higher expected quality return per premium-model call.

The routing system should consider **downstream blast radius**, not only task difficulty.

## 4. High-impact operations that normally justify premium review

The following are presumptive premium-review candidates, although measured eval evidence may later downgrade them:

### Series level
- commercial niche/positioning synthesis;
- recurring protagonist/team engine;
- multi-book character arc;
- recurring supernatural/world contract;
- Series Bible;
- book-to-book differentiation plan.

### Book level before Writing
- selected StoryDefinition;
- CaseSolution;
- suspect/secret matrix;
- clue and red-herring architecture;
- CaseTimeline when complex;
- MysticRuleSet;
- RevealPlan;
- ending;
- anti-cliche stress test;
- writing-readiness audit.

### Mid-book
- structural audit;
- solution drift check;
- predictability/fair-play diagnosis;
- character stagnation diagnosis;
- series collision review.

### Whole-book
- developmental edit;
- independent adversarial case reconstruction;
- ColdReader synthesis;
- whole-book repetition/AI-template analysis;
- final literary diagnosis;
- publication-readiness recommendation to human Owner.

## 5. Operations that should NOT default to premium

Premium routing is not justified merely because text is creative.

Do not default to the most expensive executor for:
- every scene draft;
- every paragraph rewrite;
- every dialogue line;
- chapter summaries;
- metadata extraction;
- routine continuity checks;
- simple factual lookups;
- mechanical audio cleanup;
- spelling/grammar;
- deterministic duplicate detection;
- formatting/export.

Escalate only when evidence shows the standard lane is insufficient.

## 6. Astra-class role

Astra-class long-context/high-reasoning execution is especially suitable as a candidate for operations requiring one or more of:

- simultaneous understanding of many authority objects;
- long-context whole-book analysis;
- difficult causal planning;
- subtle structural editing;
- comparison of multiple large alternatives;
- series-level semantic reasoning;
- high-risk final diagnosis.

MYSTERY OS must not encode a permanent vendor/model monopoly. “Astra-class” means the currently strongest proven executor for this operation. Routing may change when eval evidence changes.

## 7. Agents API / Codex editorial-prep role

A bounded editorial-prep agent is useful when the operation is inherently multi-step and benefits from iterative structured work rather than one response.

Good candidates:
- generating several concept architectures then self-critiquing them under explicit rules;
- constructing a CaseSolution and running internal consistency passes;
- building suspect/clue/timeline objects as a coordinated package;
- running an adversarial attack on a proposed solution;
- comparing a new series book against many prior Book Passports;
- preparing a complete writing-readiness dossier.

Agent use must remain:
- explicitly launched;
- cost-bounded;
- schema-constrained;
- proposal-only;
- human-gated.

Do not use an agent merely to turn one simple prompt into a more expensive workflow.

## 8. Standard Writer role

Once global authority is strong and a SceneContract is admitted, a standard Writer may draft the scene with only the minimum required spoiler context.

The Writer receives:
- current approved StoryDefinition subset;
- relevant character states;
- exact scene contract;
- relevant clues/secrets;
- Style Profile;
- prohibited disclosures;
- continuity constraints;
- anti-cliche warnings;
- required research facts.

It should **not** be asked to redesign the case while writing.

If the scene contract is impossible or weak, Writer returns `ARCHITECTURE_BLOCKER` rather than inventing a workaround.

## 9. Escalation triggers

A Class B operation escalates to premium when one or more are true:

- standard output repeatedly fails the same quality gate;
- scene requires reasoning across many distant book facts;
- a proposed local fix changes case truth or reveal architecture;
- character motivation cannot be repaired locally;
- prose/voice fails comparative evaluation materially;
- competing solutions cannot be distinguished by routine evaluation;
- a series collision is semantic rather than lexical;
- human editor flags “technically correct but flat/generic” output;
- the cost of a wrong decision has high downstream blast radius.

Escalation must record its reason.

## 10. De-escalation rule

If bounded evaluations show a cheaper executor matches premium quality for an operation, route that operation downward.

Examples:
- routine scene drafting may move to a cheaper model after blind comparisons;
- clue extraction may become deterministic/standard;
- line editing for a stable Style Profile may not require premium reasoning.

The system should learn where expensive intelligence is actually valuable.

## 11. Blind/pairwise quality tests

For uncertain high-impact operations, compare bounded alternatives under the same authority/context.

Examples:
- CaseSolution A vs B;
- opening architecture A vs B;
- midpoint repair A vs B;
- premium Writer vs standard Writer on 2–3 representative scenes;
- literary edit A vs B.

Human evaluation should be blind to model identity when practical.

Record:
- exact inputs;
- outputs;
- model/effort;
- cost;
- quality findings;
- human preference;
- downstream defect rates.

Do not run duplicate premium comparisons on every scene.

## 12. Quality/cost ledger

MYSTERY OS should eventually expose cost at:
- operation;
- book;
- series.

For each premium operation record:
- why premium was selected;
- estimated/reserved/confirmed cost;
- model/effort;
- output revision;
- quality result;
- whether escalation was useful.

This creates evidence for future routing rather than relying on brand intuition.

## 13. Fail-closed cost rule

No paid operation starts without current bounded authorization under BOOK OS cost policy.

If a requested premium run exceeds Owner cap:
- do not silently downgrade a quality-critical operation;
- present the trade-off;
- allow Owner to choose a lower-cost route, increase the bounded cap, postpone, or revise scope.

## 14. Example production pattern

A cost-aware premium-quality mystery may legitimately use:

1. deterministic commercial/source collection where possible;
2. premium/agent concept and Series Bible analysis;
3. premium CaseSolution + adversarial review;
4. premium high-level mystery/character architecture;
5. standard Writer for most admitted scenes;
6. deterministic/standard scene QA continuously;
7. premium midpoint audit only when the book has enough material;
8. standard local corrections where bounded;
9. premium whole-book developmental/adversarial review;
10. standard bounded rewrite passes under accepted decisions;
11. premium final literary/readiness diagnosis;
12. human Literary Master approval.

This is an example routing pattern, not a fixed mandatory model schedule.

## 15. Quality target remains invariant

Routing optimizes **cost of reaching the quality target**.

It does not redefine the target downward to fit the cheapest path.
